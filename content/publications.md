[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**, Stephen M. de Bruyn Kops, Patrice Meunier, Emmanuel Villermaux. ‘Finite-time Lyapunov exponents in conservative systems: the importance of rare compression events’ Submitted, PNAS.](Publications/Finite_time_Lyapunov_exponents.pdf)

Numerous phenomena in the tangible world are not governed by a mean but rather by significant deviations from it. For instance, odors are perceived far from their source through yet unmixed parcels of carrying fluid, and delayed spray evaporation facilitates long-distance disease transmission from human exhalations, among others. Because of a geometric constraint due to incompressibility, the tail of the low Lyapunov exponents distribution (which quantify mixing rates) are exponentially distributed, implying that large, practically unaltered concentration levels far from the source are algebraically distributed in stirred mixtures, in all dimensions of space. In chaotic and turbulent flows, these rare events have a persistent consequence on the mixture composition. Exceptions are, in fact, the rule.

[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**, Lois E. Baker, Miles M. P. Couchman, Ali Mashayek,Colm-cille P. Caulfield. ‘Potential biases in marginal instability and overturn-based approaches to the parameterisation of ocean mixing’ In prep., Geophysical Research Letters.](Publications/Applicability_stability_based_paradigm_mixing.pdf)

We develop a machine-learning framework to faithfully compare in-situ observations of ocean mixing and current parameterizations. We show that conventional marginal-stability parameterizations of ocean mixing miss the continuum of mixing in ocean interiors. The deficiency is linked to spatial biases and lack of representation of the diversity of mixing processes. Observations combined with novel data-driven methods (simulations, data assimilation, machine learning) can help identify the knowledge gaps. 


[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**. ‘The routes towards homogeneity in stratified turbulent flows’ Journal of Fluid Mechanics 1032 (2026): R2.](Publications/routes_towards_homogeneity_in_stratified_turbulent_flows.pdf)

Quantifying the rate at which a stratified turbulent flow mixes a density field is of
crucial importance for many environmental and industrial applications. Mixing is inherently related to the time evolution of the probability density function of the scalar being mixed `$p(\rho)$`. Here, using techniques from statistical mechanics and stochastic processes, we develop a model for the time-evolution of the probability distribution `$p$`, with no free parameters. The model predictions are in agreement with direct numerical simulation and known scaling laws. In particular, this approach goes beyond the conventional marginal-stability based parameterisations of mixing. See [here](../blog/posts_1/) for an overview. 

[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**. ‘Nondissipative variable density turbulence: routes toward statistical equilibrium’ Center for Turbulence Research, Annual Research Briefs, 2025.](Publications/CTR_Research_brief.pdf)

In the absence of mixing (i.e. no molecular diffusivity), the distribution of density levels `$p(\rho)$` does not evolve in time. Statistical mechanics tells us that, in that case, the collective behaviour of fluid parcels agitated by turbulent fluctuations organises, after some time, the coarse-grained density field into a stable equilibrium state of [maximum disorder](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/abs/statistical-mechanics-approach-to-mixing-in-stratified-fluids/1F764D637D30C2E6B89852CB226FE041). Here, we detail the time-evolution of the density field towards this equilibrium state. 

[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**, Stephen M. de Bruyn Kops, Colm-cille P. Caulfield.‘Modelling dispersion in stratified turbulent flows as a resetting process’ Journal of Fluid Mechanics 1002 (2025): A48.](Publications/modelling_dispersion_in_stratified_turbulent_flows_as_a_resetting_process.pdf)

Here, we show that the vertical dispersion of lagrangian tracers in stratified turbulent flows can be modelled using [diffusion with stochastic resetting](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.106.160601) and memory. The turbulent energy wants to disperse fluid parcels away from their neutrally buoyant positions. Buoyancy forces want to bring them back. The competition between these two effects limits dispersion (if no buoyancy forces were present, the lamellae will just run away forever, as in classic dispersion) and an equilibrium state can be reached. 

[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**, Miles M. P. Couchman, Ali Mashayek, Stephen M. de Bruyn Kops and Colm-cille P. Caulfield. ‘Prandtl number effects on extreme mixing events in forced stratified turbulence’ Journal of Fluid Mechanics 983 (2024): R1.](Publications/prandtl_number_effects_on_extreme_mixing_events_in_forced_stratified_turbulence.pdf) 

Using some of the most intensive [simulations](https://stratified-turbulence.github.io/web/) of stratified turbulence, we show that extreme mixing events are located in regions that are overlooked by current mixing parameterisations. 

[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**, Colm-cille P. Caulfield, Patrice Meunier, Emmanuel Villermaux. ‘Settling versus mixing in stratified shear flows’ Journal of Fluid Mechanics 974 (2023): R1.](Publications/settling_versus_mixing_in_stratified_shear_flows.pdf)

We explore the fundamental small-scale mechanisms of diffusive mixing in stratified shear flows by analysing the settling of a dense blob in a simple experimental flow geometry. We show that such mixing results from the simultaneous and competing effects of stretching-enhanced diffusion, which tends to mix fluid parcels with different densities, and restoring buoyancy forces, which tend to return vertically perturbed parcels to their initial position and hence limit mixing. 

[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**, Ali Mashayek, and Colm-cille P. Caulfield. ‘Turbulent disruption of density staircases in stratified shear flows.’ Journal of Fluid Mechanics 961 (2023): A30.](Publications/turbulent_disruption_of_density_staircases_in_stratified_shear_flows.pdf)

The formation of step-like ‘density staircase’ distributions induced by stratification and turbulence has been widely studied. However, such staircases are not often observed in ocean interiors, except in regions where double-diffusion processes are important. Using reduced-order modelling and stability analysis, we describe the relevant regime parameters in which staircase formation is expected.
