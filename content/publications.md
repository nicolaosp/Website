[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**, Stephen M. de Bruyn Kops, Patrice Meunier, Emmanuel Villermaux. ‘Finite-time Lyapunov exponents in conservative systems: the importance of rare compression events’ Submitted, PNAS.](Publications/Finite_time_Lyapunov_exponents.pdf)

**Numerous phenomena in the tangible world are not governed by a mean but rather by significant deviations from it.** For instance, odors are perceived far from their source through yet unmixed parcels of carrying fluid, and delayed spray evaporation facilitates long-distance disease transmission from human exhalations, among others. Because of a geometric constraint due to incompressibility, the tail of the low Lyapunov exponents distribution (which quantify mixing rates) are exponentially distributed, implying that large, practically unaltered concentration levels far from the source are algebraically distributed in stirred mixtures, in all dimensions of space. In chaotic and turbulent flows, these rare events have a persistent consequence on the mixture composition. **Exceptions are, in fact, the rule.**

[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**, Lois E. Baker, Miles M. P. Couchman, Ali Mashayek,Colm-cille P. Caulfield. ‘Potential biases in marginal instability and overturn-based approaches to the parameterisation of ocean mixing’ In prep., Geophysical Research Letters.](Publications/Applicability_stability_based_paradigm_mixing.pdf)

We develop a **machine-learning framework to faithfully compare in-situ observations of ocean mixing and current parameterizations.** We show that conventional marginal-stability parameterizations of ocean mixing miss the continuum of mixing in ocean interiors. The deficiency is linked to spatial biases and lack of representation of the diversity of mixing processes. Observations combined with novel data-driven methods (simulations, data assimilation, machine learning) can help identify the knowledge gaps. 


[**Nicolaos Petropoulos**. ‘The routes towards homogeneity in stratified turbulent flows’ Journal of Fluid Mechanics 1032 (2026): R2.](Publications/routes_towards_homogeneity_in_stratified_turbulent_flows.pdf)

Quantifying the rate at which a stratified turbulent flow mixes a density field is of
crucial importance for many environmental and industrial applications. Mixing is inherently related to the time evolution of the **probability density function** of the scalar being mixed `$p(\rho)$`. Here, using techniques from statistical mechanics and stochastic processes, we develop a model for the time-evolution of the probability distribution `$p$`, with no free parameters. The model predictions are in agreement with direct numerical simulation and known scaling laws. In particular, **this approach goes beyond the conventional marginal-stability based parameterisations of mixing**. See [posts](../blog/posts_1/) for more details.
